# Zoo Decision Atlas

This is the canonical hierarchical record of **owner-approved product direction**, seeded from the [ten agreed product choices](../zoo-product-experience.md), [Zoo architecture](../architecture.md) and [roadmap #20](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/20). Topic headings are navigation; semantic leaves are exactly **Decisions** or **Questions**. Preserve each dotted ID. Do not treat an existing implementation as owner approval for a new product requirement.

## Binary implementation evidence

Nested `- [x]` items identify **only the precisely cited implemented/tested capability**; `- [ ]` identifies the specific missing or unverified remainder. No other progress states. A checked technical foundation **does not imply full decision/product acceptance**, and a Decision without a checklist is *unaudited*, not 0%. An issue is temporary execution state; the Atlas is permanent product intent. Use repository-relative `path::symbol` plus tests/immutable evidence instead of fragile source line numbers. The current `daq` parser does not yet index nested checkboxes; see [coding-tooling #322](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/coding-tooling/issues/322) and [#323](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/coding-tooling/issues/323).

  - **1.6** Audience and emotional core
    - **1.6.1** [decision] Zoo's guiding player fantasy is **a personally expressive animal-centered game for an audience including girls** (owner clarification, 2026-11-11): offer **many different, appealingly cute species**, memorable individual animals that players can identify with and feel attached to, and a zoo that reflects the player's own taste. Prioritize **delight, discovery, expression and attachment** over fine-grained economic balance, speed, high scores or demanding optimization. Management and welfare stay real, legible and recoverable, but should serve creative play rather than dominate it. Cute means expressive, recognizable species personalities, readable behavior and visual warmth—not assumed gender stereotypes, compulsory pink coloring or interchangeable reskins. The exact means of personalization and expanded 1.0 species count need further steering (Questions 1.6.2, 6.1.9). ([#216](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/216), [#112](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/112), [#100](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/100), [#119](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/119))
    - **1.6.2** [question] Which **player activity** should first prove Zoo's personal-expression fantasy: designing beautiful habitats/park themes, caring for and bonding with individual animals, or discovering and collecting many distinct species? The choice prioritizes a first complete playable slice, not exclusion of the other loops.
    - **1.6.3** [decision] Answers 1.6.2 (owner, 2026-11-11): **first prioritize bonding with individual animals (option B), then creative building and habitat/park decoration (option A)**. Start by making named, identifiable, cute animals feel alive and worth following and caring about through real behavior; then make it satisfying to design personally distinctive places for them. **Species discovery/collection remains a core long-term pillar**, not rejected or removed; its exact roster and launch breadth remain open (6.1.9). Verify these experiences as playable, emotionally engaging journeys, not just technical pick/camera or placement demos. This orders work under [#216](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/216), using [#141](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/141), [#112](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/112) and [#100](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/100); it does **not yet approve** petting, gifting, player-avatar customization, a relationship meter or specific personality algorithms.
    - **1.6.4** [question] What kind of *bonding interaction* should be the first real play loop: watch/name/discover personalities through behavior; direct gentle care and play interactions; or deeper ongoing individual relationships and memories? Choose a slice with emotional payoff and real persistent individual identity before expanding its mechanics.
    - **1.6.5** [decision] Answers 1.6.4 (owner option B and further clarification, 2026-11-11): **Zoo is now a keeper-first animal-bonding and creative zoo game, not primarily a management/economic optimization simulator**. Players should be able to **play as a zoo keeper**, inspect individual cute animals in real habitats, name them and **interact directly through species-appropriate feeding and hands-on care/play**, with meaningful reactions and real care state. Gentle petting/touch is an attractive candidate **only for suitable animals and with appropriate access/safety**; it is not universal, especially not for big cats or dangerous species. Animal identity and welfare come from `zoo-core`; scene/input/presentation use existing shared authorities. Management, guests, staff, finance and scenarios may support the zoo, but must no longer dominate the fantasy or 1.0 acceptance. The earlier focus on a finished management game (6.1.1) is superseded **as product priority**, while exact survival/timing of its scenario commitments remains an open scope question. ([#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217), [#216](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/216), [#141](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/141))
    - **1.6.6** [question] What **keeper control/camera experience** should support direct animal care: remain top-down with an in-world controlled keeper, switch between park overview/building and an immersive keeper mode, or play mainly as an in-world third-person keeper with building tools? Resolve before implementing a new avatar/movement/control stack. ([#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217))
    - **1.6.7** [decision] Answers 1.6.6 (owner hybrid direction, 2026-11-11): **retain the zoo-building/design overview and let the player seamlessly “enter” an actual habitat to play as a keeper and interact closely with its individual animals**. The two experiences are complementary: design an expressive home, enter it, inspect/name/feed/care for suitable animals, observe their cute reactions, then return to building **without starting a disconnected mini-game or losing the persistent park**. Use the same authoritative `zoo-core` animals, habitat geometry, access and save state. Specific near-camera/locomotion perspective (first- vs third-person) is an implementation interaction detail to verify with actual desktop/touch play; this decision does not commit to a new engine or universal petting. ([#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217), [#100](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/100), [#216](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/216))
    - **1.6.8** [question] How should players express themselves through the keeper character in habitat mode: customizable named appearance/outfit, a fixed friendly keeper character, or minimal/no visible keeper identity focused on the animals? This is separate from the already approved animal naming and bonding.
    - **1.6.9** [decision] Answers 1.6.8 (owner option A, 2026-11-11): the **playable keeper has full, friendly identity customization**—the player chooses the keeper's **name, appearance, hairstyle and clothing**, with attractive compatible looks, colors and more outfits obtainable through relaxed play. Customize the *same character* seen in real habitat interactions and preserve selections across mode switches and saves. Provide expressive choices immediately; earning cosmetics must not require difficult scoring, punitive finances, mandatory campaign completion or a grind (Decision 6.1.12), and clothing never changes animal-care ability/stats. Reuse the existing appearance/rig/wardrobe composition capabilities: Zoo owns option IDs, selection UX and save state ([#218](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/218)); [asset-tooling #591](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/asset-tooling/issues/591) owns the stylized keeper kit and compatible authored parts, with shared animation/rendering through `3d-lab`. Full player-facing customization does not require unlimited morph sliders or a generic avatar marketplace.
    - **1.6.10** [question] How **persistent** should individual animals' bond with the keeper become: immediate cute reactions to care, an ongoing individual familiarity/trust relation, or deeper personal stories and lasting events? Keep the answer species-appropriate, accessible and save-stable. ([#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217), [#216](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/216))
    - **1.6.11** [decision] Answers 1.6.10 (owner option B, 2026-11-11): **each individual animal develops a persistent relationship with the player's keeper** through kind, species-appropriate care. Animals can grow familiar, remember interactions at a useful bounded level, express individual preferences and respond differently to a familiar keeper; greetings and care reactions should visibly reflect this **in the actual habitat**. Store and validate identity/preferences/familiarity in `zoo-core` across save/reload and building↔keeper switching; presentation and audio follow authoritative state and reuse existing animal assets/animation owners. Bonding is **gentle, non-punitive and not a grind** (Decision 6.1.12): no mandatory daily relationship maintenance, harsh decay, locked core animal interaction or score advantage. Rich authored personal story arcs remain an optional later direction, not implied by choosing B. ([#219](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/219), [#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217))
    - **1.6.12** [question] How should the player **discover and acquire many new cute species** in this keeper-first, negligible-difficulty zoo: freely choose animals from a welcoming adoption catalog, rescue/help animals through short activities, or combine immediate easy adoption with optional rescue/discovery experiences? The answer should preserve animal variety and creative freedom without management grinding. ([#110](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/110), [#119](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/119))
    - **1.6.13** [decision] Answers 1.6.12 (owner option C, 2026-11-11): **combine immediately accessible animal adoption with optional gentle rescue/discovery encounters**. The default creative/keeper game offers a welcoming adoption catalog for supported species once a suitable habitat exists; ordinary animal choice, care, decorative building, and keeper bonding do not depend on money grind, long wait, rescue quest completion or challenge success. Short voluntary help/discovery activities can introduce animals through meaningful keeper interactions, with charming reactions and optional arrivals, but do **not** gate essential species, create mandatory story progression, or duplicate animal identities/rewards across saves. `zoo-core` owns acquisition and persistent identity (#110); #220 owns a first optional playable encounter; #119 owns collection breadth and #216 owns delightful keeper experience. Scenario-specific economic constraints may still exist in optional separate challenge modes (6.1.12), not the welcoming default.
    - **1.6.14** [question] What should **playing with animals inside their habitats** feel like beyond feeding and petting? Animals independently use suitable toys/enrichment while the keeper observes (A); the keeper mainly initiates playful interactions (B); or both animals and keeper can initiate species-appropriate play (C)? Keep behavior cute, safe and non-grindy, with habitats and animals remaining alive without constant player intervention. ([#107](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/107), [#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217), [#100](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/100))
    - **1.6.15** [decision] Answers 1.6.14 (owner option B, 2026-11-11): **keeper-initiated activities are the primary way players play with animals** beyond feeding/petting. In a real habitat, the player deliberately offers a suitable toy/enrichment item or initiates a gentle action and receives a cute, individually recognizable reaction. Species suitability, contact safety and animal preferences matter; actions use validated zoo-core events (#217, #221), purposeful behaviors (#107) and persistent bonds (#219), with browser presentation never inventing outcomes. Animals **remain naturally active** without keeper input (walking, eating, resting, exploring, socializing and using enrichment as supported by #107); choosing B sets the primary *player-interaction emphasis*, not an inert zoo. No score, repetition grind, forced minigame, failure punishment or daily upkeep (6.1.12). Reuse #100 for authored habitat toys and shared 3d-lab/asset-tooling for animations, preserving existing architecture.
    - **1.6.16** [question] How should the keeper **perform** simple animal play activities on desktop and touch: one-step contextual actions with lovely animations (A), direct tactile interactions such as gently dragging a brush or toy (B), or simple one-step actions with optional tactile gestures when they add delight (C)? Keep controls easy and forgiving, never timing- or precision-scored. ([#221](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/221), [#217](https://github.com/moenarch-games/zoo/issues/217))
    - **1.6.17** [decision] Answers 1.6.16 (owner option C, 2026-11-11): **keeper animal-play actions have a simple click/tap path, with optional tactile interactions when genuinely delightful**. Every supported play/care activity remains usable through a forgiving contextual action without timing, precision or motor-skill pressure; optional drag/gesture experiences (such as guiding a toy or brushing a suitable animal) may enrich the same activity but cannot grant exclusive access, better bonding rewards or block desktop/touch accessibility. Preserve real zoo-core gameplay commands, species safety, stable animal identity and preference/relationship events (#221/#217/#219); browser gestures are presentation/input alternatives, not parallel simulation or extra mini-games. Support touch and mouse, cancellation, interruptions and accessible non-drag fallback. Start with one verified species/activity, then expand from play evidence. (6.1.12)
    - **1.6.18** [question] How much should **baby animals and family life** contribute to cozy keeper gameplay: occasional cute babies with a simplified lifecycle (A), substantial parent/offspring relationships and visible growth (B), or optional family/growth experiences that players may enable or disable in relaxed play (C)? The keeper can know, care for and bond with an animal across life stages without losing its name, identity, preferences or familiar relationship on growth, habitat changes or save/reload. Authoritative breeding, ancestry, age and lifecycle remain in zoo-core (#110); behavior and parent/offspring interactions build on #107 and visual/content kits on #119. Show appealing natural interactions and accessible family views (see #222 UI design). Keep the default experience cozy and negligibly difficult (6.1.12): no mandatory breeding timetables, hard population optimization, pressure to separate families, abrupt stress penalties or reward grind. Existing sandbox settings for pausing aging/breeding remain compatible but are **not** a substitute for meaningful default family-life gameplay. Do not promise genetics simulation or perpetual realistic husbandry.
